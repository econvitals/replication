# Release-Day Estimate

**On econvitals.org:** the asterisked rows of the inflation tables on the [home page](https://econvitals.org/#inflation); [Inflation › PCE](https://econvitals.org/inflation/#pce) and [› CPI](https://econvitals.org/inflation/#cpi) (table rows and the trimmed-mean charts); the [CPI](https://econvitals.org/inflation/cpidetail/) and [PCE](https://econvitals.org/inflation/pcedetail/) inflation detail pages (the median charts). Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#release-day-estimate).

**What it is.** The Cleveland Fed (median CPI, 16% trimmed-mean CPI, median PCE) and the Dallas Fed (trimmed-mean PCE) publish their measures hours or a day after the BLS and BEA releases. Econvitals rebuilds each one from the component detail the agency publishes at 8:30 a.m. — 45 CPI components with the Cleveland Fed’s weights, 177 PCE components with BEA’s spending weights — using the banks’ published recipes: a weighted median, or trimming the fastest- and slowest-rising tails of the weight distribution. Each chart and table row is one spliced series: the bank’s official figure for every month it has published, the Econvitals estimate only for the newest month, marked with an asterisk in the tables and faded bars on the charts and replaced automatically when the official number posts. Only the newest month is ever an Econvitals estimate, and it can differ from the official print by up to about 0.1 percentage point.

**How the script does it.** It rebuilds all four measures for every month since 2015:
- *CPI (Cleveland Fed recipe).* Each of the 45 components’ one-month change in its seasonally adjusted index; the four regional owners’ equivalent rent indexes, which BLS publishes only unadjusted, are adjusted here. Weights: the Cleveland Fed’s current component weights, rolled back through history with each component’s unadjusted index and renormalized each month. Median = the change at the 50th percentile of the weight distribution; trimmed mean = drop 8% of the weight from each end, splitting the component that straddles each cut. A month needs at least 43 of the 45 components.
- *PCE (Dallas Fed recipe).* BEA’s 177 components, each weighted by its Fisher spending share (the average of its share at last month’s prices with this month’s and last month’s quantities); drop the lowest 24% and the highest 31% of the weight. The median PCE estimate is the 50th percentile of the same distribution; the Cleveland Fed’s own component set differs slightly.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (needs free `BEA_API_KEY` and `FRED_API_KEY`). Writes one file per chart, `output/median-cpi.csv`, `output/trimmed-mean-cpi.csv`, `output/median-pce.csv` and `output/trimmed-mean-pce.csv`, each with the chart’s two columns from the rebuild alone — `chg_1m_ann`, the one-month change at an annual rate (the bars), and `chg_3m_ann`, the three-month change at an annual rate (the line) — and beside them the bank’s figure (`official_1m_ann`), and the one-month changes not annualized (`ours_1m`, `official_1m`). Under ten seconds once the data are cached.

**Track record.** `python history.py --data-repo PATH`, where PATH is a git clone of Econvitals’ public data repository (github.com/econvitals/econvitals-data), rebuilds `history/release-day.csv`. Each chart’s data file there is versioned, so its history shows what the site showed and when: the estimate on release morning, then the bank’s figure. One row per measure and month, once the bank’s figure has replaced the estimate, from July 2026 (the files’ history starts in August 2026).

**Data.**
- BLS bulk files at download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/cu (the U.S. city average topic files): the 41 national CPI components, seasonally adjusted, and their unadjusted twins for the weights.
- FRED: the four regional owners’ equivalent rent indexes (`CUUR0100SEHC`, `CUUR0200SEHC`, `CUUR0300SEHC`, `CUUR0400SEHC`), and for comparison the Cleveland Fed’s median and trimmed-mean CPI (`MEDCPIM157SFRBCLE`, `TRMMEANCPIM157SFRBCLE`) and the Dallas Fed’s trimmed-mean PCE (`PCETRIM1M158SFRBDAL`).
- The Cleveland Fed’s current median CPI component weights and its median PCE history (both CSV files on clevelandfed.org).
- BEA, NIPA underlying detail (dataset `NIUnderlyingDetail`): Tables 2.4.4U (`U20404`) and 2.4.5U (`U20405`).
- `inputs/cleveland_median_cpi_components.csv` (the 45 CPI components) and `inputs/dallas_trimmed_mean_components.csv` (the 177 PCE components, with BEA line and series code).

**How close.** Compared every night with the four published charts over the latest 24 months, where every month is the bank’s own figure: the rebuild is checked against what the banks then published. In the charts’ annualized units the tolerances are 0.006 percentage point for trimmed-mean PCE’s one-month change and 0.004 for its three-month change (the Dallas Fed rounds its published rate to two decimals; the rebuild itself is within 0.0002 point a month on average since 2015); 0.3 and 0.2 for trimmed-mean CPI; 0.6 and 0.25 for median CPI; 0.6 and 0.2 for median PCE. In the banks’ own one-month units, the average difference since 2015 is 0.0002 percentage point for trimmed-mean PCE, 0.012 for trimmed-mean CPI, 0.015 for median CPI and 0.009 for median PCE. A median is one component’s change, so when two components sit near the middle of the weight distribution a small difference in weights or seasonal factors picks the neighbor: that is where the larger worst-month gaps (about 0.05 point a month) come from. For the CPI measures the remaining gap is the Cleveland Fed’s own seasonal adjustment of regional rent and its weights, which it publishes only for the current month.

Econvitals’ production code adjusts the regional rent indexes with the Census Bureau’s X-13ARIMA-SEATS. This script uses the simple stand-in in `common/tracking.py` instead: measured on November 8, 2026, it lands nearly as close to the Cleveland Fed on average (median CPI 0.015 against 0.012 with X-13) and closer in the worst recent month (0.047 against 0.106), and it needs nothing installed. So on release morning, when the site’s newest month is the production estimate, this script’s figure for that month can differ from the site’s by a few hundredths of a point on the CPI measures; the PCE measures need no seasonal adjustment and match.
