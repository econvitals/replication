# Trade Balance Excluding Data-Center Goods

**On econvitals.org:** [Other › Trade detail](https://econvitals.org/other/tradedetail/), the whole page, both views of its Percent of GDP | Dollars switch: the year-to-date balance with and without data-center goods, the goods balance without them, the stacked bars, imports of data-center goods against everything else, and the change in the balance since 2024 by component. Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#data-center-trade).

**What it is.** The U.S. trade balance split into data-center goods and everything else. Data-center goods are four Census end-use categories: computers (where AI servers are counted), computer parts and accessories (the boards that hold AI chips), semiconductors, and telecommunications equipment. Net exports of the four come from BEA’s quarterly international transactions accounts, which match Census’s five-digit end-use figures, with each quarter split evenly across its months; the months after BEA’s latest quarter come from Census’s monthly trade release (the latest month and the one before as printed, any month between as an equal share of what the year-to-date total leaves). Everything else is the total goods and services balance less those net exports. Each year’s bar covers the same months, January through the latest month published. The percent-of-GDP figures divide by GDP over the same months, with quarterly GDP put on its middle month, interpolated log-linearly between quarters and extended past the latest quarter at its latest growth. The change chart compares the latest year with 2024 and its pieces add up to the total; gold there is net exports of nonmonetary gold (HS 7108 plus 7115), and “all other goods” is the goods balance less gold and data-center goods.

The four categories are a proxy: they carry consumer computers as well as data-center equipment, and miss data-center gear counted elsewhere, such as power and cooling equipment. Months inside a BEA quarter are an equal third of it, which is exact for any window ending in March, June, September or December and a close share otherwise.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (needs free `FRED_API_KEY` and `BEA_API_KEY`; the gold bar of the change chart also needs a free `CENSUS_API_KEY`, and without one the script skips that chart and writes the rest). Writes `output/ytd.csv` (each year’s January-to-latest-month balance in billions of dollars and percent of GDP: total, data-center goods, everything else, goods excluding data-center goods), `output/imports_index.csv` (quarterly, 2024 Q4 = 100) and `output/change_since_2024.csv`.

**Data.**
- BEA international transactions, table 2.1, quarterly, seasonally adjusted, from the BEA API: indicators `ExpGdsComp`, `ImpGdsComp`, `ExpGdsCompAccPeriAndParts`, `ImpGdsCompAccPeriAndParts`, `ExpGdsSemiconductors`, `ImpGdsSemiconductors`, `ExpGdsTelecomEquip`, `ImpGdsTelecomEquip`.
- Census FT-900, the current release’s exhibits 7 and 8 (exports and imports by end-use category, seasonally adjusted), `census.gov/foreign-trade/Press-Release/current_press_release/exh7.xlsx` and `exh8.xlsx`.
- Census FT-900 exhibit 1 history (total, goods and services balances and goods imports, seasonally adjusted, monthly from 1992), `census.gov/foreign-trade/statistics/historical/exhibit_history.xlsx`. FRED carries the same series (`BOPGSTB`, `BOPGTB`, `BOPSTB`, `BOPGIMP`), but its copies of 1999–2011 and 2016 have not taken some of Census’s revisions (up to $1.2 billion a month in 1999–2000), so the script reads Census’s own workbook.
- Nominal GDP: FRED `GDP` (BEA table 1.1.5).
- Nonmonetary gold, HS 7108 and 7115, monthly, not seasonally adjusted: the Census international trade API (`api.census.gov/data/timeseries/intltrade/exports/hs` and `imports/hs`), with the FT-900 exhibits’ “Nonmonetary gold” line for a month the API has not reached yet.

**How close.** Reproduces every published series. The site rounds dollar figures to a tenth of a billion, shares of GDP to four decimals, the import index to two and the change chart to a tenth of a billion and two decimals of GDP; the nightly check allows half of the last published decimal (plus a hair for floating point) on each, and every series sits inside it. The total balance is compared with the plain year-to-date balance charts (`trade-balance-ytd`), because the stacked bars’ total is the sum of two separately rounded parts. Census replaces the current exhibits with each monthly release, so for the few hours between Census’s release and the site’s refresh the newest month can differ.
