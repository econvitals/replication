# Econvitals Composite Activity and Inflation Indicators

**On econvitals.org:** [Home › How Is the Economy Doing, and How Surprising Has It Been?](https://econvitals.org/#surprise), the 2-by-2 grid and the two charts. Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#composite-indicators).

**What it is.** Four weekly indices on two axes, activity and inflation, each read two ways. Positive always means more activity or more inflation.

- The **absolute** reading compares each indicator’s latest estimate with its own history since 2013: subtract the typical (median) reading over 2013 through the year before, divide by the typical spread (a robust standard deviation, 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation), cap at four, then average with balanced weights (the two themes on each axis count equally, and the indicators equally within a theme).
- The **surprise** reading does the same for each release’s first print against the consensus forecast that preceded it, scaled by the typical size of that indicator’s surprises, with weights set by how much the 2-year Treasury yield has historically moved per surprise.

Fourteen indicators go in: payrolls, the unemployment rate (inverted), job openings, real GDP, the retail control group, personal income, housing starts and consumer sentiment on the activity side; core PCE, core CPI, core PPI, the employment cost index, average hourly earnings and one-year inflation expectations on the inflation side. The three core price indices use the 12-month change on the absolute side (and the monthly change on the surprise side). Each Friday’s value is a decayed average of the recent readings: each carries its weight times exp(−age / 45 days), so the half-life is about a month; readings stop counting after 185 days, surprises after 120. On the absolute side a reading is dated by the month or quarter it describes.

**What this folder rebuilds: the absolute half.** The surprise half is not rebuilt. It needs, for every release since 2013, the consensus forecast that stood before it. Econvitals takes those from the Nasdaq economic calendar, which is a free web page but not a documented data feed and keeps no public archive of past consensus values, and for the latest weeks from a source that is not public. Econvitals does not republish that archive, so a reader cannot reproduce the surprise lines.

**Licensed inputs: none.** None of the fourteen indicators comes from the Conference Board, ISM or S&P Global. Consumer sentiment and one-year inflation expectations are the University of Michigan’s, read from its Surveys of Consumers tables (FRED’s copies, `UMCSENT` and `MICH`, are the same numbers but run about a month behind at Michigan’s request).

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (needs a free `FRED_API_KEY`). Writes `output/absolute.csv`: the weekly activity and inflation readings from January 2015.

**Data.** FRED: `PAYEMS` (monthly change), `UNRATE`, `JTSJOL`, `A191RL1Q225SBEA` (real GDP growth, annual rate), `RSAFS` less `RSMVPD`, `RSGASS`, `RSBMGESD` and `RSFSDP` (the retail control group, monthly percent change), `PI` (monthly percent change), `HOUST`, `PCEPILFE`, `CPILFENS` and `PPICOR` (12-month changes), `ECIALLCIV` (quarterly percent change) and `CES0500000003` (12-month change). University of Michigan: `sca.isr.umich.edu/files/tbmics.csv` (column `ICS_ALL`) and `tbmpx1px5.csv` (column `PX_MD`). Changes step through the observations as published, as the published index does. One consequence: there is no October 2025 core CPI (BLS missed it in the shutdown), so from November 2025 through October 2026 the core CPI “12-month” change actually spans 13 months (September 2026: 2.82 percent, against 2.56 over 12 months). The script keeps that, to match the site; it corrects itself with the November 2026 CPI.

**How close.** Reproduces the published absolute readings exactly over their whole history: checked every night to within 0.0006 standard deviation (the site rounds to three decimals). One detail matters for the match. Econvitals rebuilds the index on Friday mornings, and its FRED inputs are the ones posted through the day before, so a report released at 8:30 that Friday (the jobs report, for one) enters the following week’s rebuild. The script therefore reads FRED as it stood on the Thursday before the latest Friday (FRED keeps every past vintage). Run it after 10:30 a.m. Eastern on a Friday, or on any later day of the week; run on a Friday morning before Econvitals’ rebuild, it gets a week ahead of the site.
