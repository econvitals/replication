# Econvitals PCE Tracking Estimate

**On econvitals.org:** [Inflation › PCE](https://econvitals.org/inflation/#pce), the lead line, the Actual/Tracker toggle and the tracker charts, only between the month’s CPI release and BEA’s PCE release. Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#pce-tracking-estimate).

**What it is.** BEA builds the PCE price index from about 220 detailed components, almost all of them priced with a BLS index that is public before BEA publishes: the CPI for most of spending, producer prices for health care, airfares, financial services and insurance, and import prices for foreign travel. Econvitals reproduces BEA’s assembly from BEA’s own published map of sources. On CPI day the CPI pins down about 71% of core PCE; the PPI lifts that to about 85%; import prices finish it. The roughly one-tenth of core PCE that BEA imputes with no monthly source is filled with its own 12-month average, the pieces are weighted with last month’s spending shares, and a small allowance is made for the gap between BEA’s first print and its revised history. It is an Econvitals estimate, not BEA’s, and the median and trimmed-mean rows inside it are estimates too.

**How the script does it.** For each of the last 36 months, and for each stage (after the CPI; after the CPI and PPI; after all three), using only BEA data through the month before:

1. *Translate.* Each component’s one-month change is the change in its BLS source, or in a blend of several whose weights are fitted (non-negative least squares) to BEA’s own history of the component. Where BEA seasonally adjusts an unadjusted BLS index itself (“SA by BEA”), the script adjusts it too and then adds an *implied seasonal factor*: the average, for the same calendar month over the past five years, of BEA’s published change less the translated one. Two components BEA smooths with a “judgmental trend” (spectator sports, tax preparation) are translated through a five-year regression on their CPI; a few whose CPI item BLS has stopped publishing borrow the parent item through the same kind of regression.
2. *Fill.* A component whose source is not out yet, or that has no monthly source at all (nonprofits, bank services furnished without payment, portfolio management, life insurance, foreign travel and a few others), gets its own average change over the past 12 months.
3. *Add up.* Components are weighted by last month’s spending shares. Once producer prices are in, 1.4 basis points come off core (1.7 off core ex housing and core ex housing and used cars, 2.1 off core services ex housing): the average amount by which this translation has run above BEA’s first print. Headline, market-based core, the median and the trimmed mean carry no offset. The Dallas Fed trimmed mean (24% trimmed from the bottom of the weight distribution, 31% from the top) and the median are taken over the Dallas Fed’s 177 components.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (needs a free `BEA_API_KEY`; the BLS indexes come from BLS’s public bulk files, which need no key). Writes `output/pce_tracking.csv`, one row per month and stage (`record` is, for example, “2026-09 post_import”): `overall`, `core` (offset included, as shown), `core_before_offset`, `core_ex_housing`, `core_ex_housing_used`, `core_services_ex_housing`, `market_based_core`, `median`, `trimmed_mean` (all one-month percent changes, not annualized), `known_share_core` (the share of core translated from released data), and `bea_overall` and `bea_core`, BEA’s own figures for the month as published today. BEA uses the Census Bureau’s X-13ARIMA-SEATS for its adjustments; if you have it (free from census.gov), set `X13PATH` to the folder holding the `x13as` program and the script uses it. Without it the script uses a simple stand-in (see below). The run takes under a minute with the stand-in, two with X-13.

**Track record.** `python history.py --state-dir PATH` (needs `FRED_API_KEY`) rebuilds `history/pce-tracking.csv` from the production tracker’s records: every estimate made with the CPI in hand, beside BEA’s first print and the miss. A month appears only after BEA has published it (the test is ALFRED, the St. Louis Fed’s archive of what was published when), and only records made before BEA’s 8:30 a.m. release count. `--backtest-file` also writes `history/pce-tracking-backtest.csv`, the production engine’s simulated real-time back-test.

**Data.**
- BEA, NIPA underlying detail (dataset `NIUnderlyingDetail`): Table 2.4.4U (`U20404`, price indexes by product) and Table 2.4.5U (`U20405`, spending by product).
- BLS bulk files at download.bls.gov/pub/time.series: the CPI’s U.S. city average topic files (`cu.data.1`, `.2`, `.11` to `.18`, `.20`), the PPI industry files for apparel, air transportation, ambulatory health care, hospitals, nursing care, finance, insurance carriers, professional services, administrative support and accommodation (`pc.data.8`, `.36`, `.47`, `.50`, `.51`, `.57`, `.58`, `.63`, `.67`, `.71`), two PPI commodity files (`wp.data.4`, `wp.data.30`) and the import price services file (`ei.data.10`).
- `inputs/pce_leaf_map.csv`: the 220 components (“leaves”) whose signed spending sums to total PCE, each with its BEA line and codes, its BLS source series and how BEA seasonally adjusts it, from BEA’s Table 2.4.4U-D, “Price Indexes for PCE: Source Data” (the September 30, 2026 edition), with BEA’s blends spelled out and the components BEA imputes marked. The flag columns say which leaves are housing, services and used vehicles and which BEA leaves out of market-based PCE.
- `inputs/dallas_trimmed_mean_components.csv`: the Dallas Fed’s 177 components, each mapped to the leaves beneath it.

**How close.** Against the estimates Econvitals actually recorded at the time (`history/pce-tracking.csv`), checked every night within 0.045 percentage point on core before the offset and 0.04 on headline. Two things separate a rerun from the record, and neither is the method:
- *The seasonal adjustment of the unadjusted PPI inputs.* With X-13 installed the script reproduces the production engine exactly: on November 8, 2026, given the same data, both put September 2026 core at 0.335 after producer prices and 0.343 after all three releases, headline at 0.507 and 0.514. The stand-in adjusts airfares and a few other services differently, which moved core by up to 3.8 basis points (mean 0.7) over the last three years. That is what the tolerance covers.
- *BEA’s revision of the month before.* The record was made with BEA’s data as it stood that week; a rerun uses today’s. For September 2026 this accounts for about half a basis point.

Measured against BEA’s current history over the last 36 months, the rerun misses core by an average of 4.0 basis points after the CPI, 3.6 after the PPI and 3.5 with all three in (4.0, 3.5 and 3.2 with X-13). Against BEA’s *first print*, in the production engine’s real-time back-test (each month run from the BEA vintage available before that month’s CPI, 2017–2026; `history/pce-tracking-backtest.csv`), the average misses on core are 5.9, 4.7 and 4.8 basis points; most of the extra is the first print itself, which sits about 5 basis points from BEA’s eventual revised figure.

**What the script leaves out.** The production engine also handles a morning when producer prices arrive before the CPI (it fills gasoline from EIA’s pump prices); that estimate is never shown, so it is not here. It translates the months through July 2026 with the sources BEA used before its September 2026 update (portfolio management, legal services and software), which matters only for scoring old first prints. And it reports every BEA aggregate, not just the eight concepts above.
