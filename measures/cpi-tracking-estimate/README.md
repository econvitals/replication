# Econvitals CPI Tracking Estimate

**On econvitals.org:** not shown. The estimate is made a few days before each CPI release and kept private until BLS publishes; this folder publishes the method and, once each month is out, the record. (It is not yet on the [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/) page.)

**What it is.** A forecast of what BLS will print for the coming month, for twelve cuts: all items, core, core goods, core services, shelter, core services ex shelter, core ex shelter, core ex shelter and used cars, food, energy, and the Cleveland Fed’s median and 16% trimmed-mean CPI.

- *The basket.* 49 pieces (`inputs/cpi_pieces.csv`) whose relative-importance weights add to 99.9% of the CPI. Each cut is the sum of its pieces at last month’s weights. Rebuilding published core from the published pieces lands within about 1.7 basis points, so the arithmetic is not where the misses are. Household operations, which BLS stopped publishing in 2024, is backed out of its parent.
- *Candidates.* Each piece gets every candidate estimate each month. From its own past: its 3-, 6-, 12- and 24-month average change, its 12-month median, the 12-month average plus half its usual calendar-month pattern, and a pull back toward the 12-month average by however much last month’s gap has tended to persist. From outside series, for four pieces: motor fuel and fuel oil, a regression on the month’s average retail gasoline or diesel price from EIA, less the usual gap between the unadjusted and adjusted change for that calendar month; utility (piped) gas, the Henry Hub price one to three months back; rent and owners’ equivalent rent, a regression on their own 3- and 12-month pace and the reading six months back.
- *Selection.* Each month a piece uses whichever candidate has had the smallest average miss over the previous 48 months (at least 24 scored), using nothing from that month or later. Until enough months are scored, the 12-month average.
- *Median and trimmed mean.* The Cleveland Fed’s 45 components are estimated the same way and sorted at the Cleveland Fed’s weights (its current weights rolled back with each component’s unadjusted index); the four regional owners’ equivalent rent indexes, which BLS publishes only unadjusted, are seasonally adjusted with the simple stand-in in `common/tracking.py`.

Used-car prices from Manheim are not used: Cox Automotive’s terms do not permit automated collection, and the estimate runs on public data only. Tried and dropped because they lost out of sample: Zillow market rents for rent and owners’ equivalent rent, gasoline for airfares, a calendar-month correction to the total, and averaging with the Cleveland Fed’s nowcast.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (needs free `FRED_API_KEY` and `EIA_API_KEY`; the CPI indexes and weights come from BLS’s public bulk files). It back-casts every month since 2015 that BLS has published, each from what was known before it, and writes `output/cpi_tracking.csv`: the twelve estimates (one-month percent changes, seasonally adjusted, not annualized) and, beside each, what was then published (`<cut>_published`: BLS’s figure; for core services ex shelter, the same pieces summed; for the median and trimmed mean, the Cleveland Fed’s). It stops at the newest month BLS has published; it never writes an estimate of a month that is not yet out. About half a minute.

**Track record.** `python history.py --state-dir PATH` (needs `FRED_API_KEY`) rebuilds `history/cpi-tracking.csv` from the production tracker’s records, one row per month per day the estimate was made, beside the first print and the miss. A month appears only after BLS has published it (the test is ALFRED, the St. Louis Fed’s archive of what was published when), and only records made before the 8:30 a.m. release count. The first month on the record is October 2026, released November 10, 2026; until then the file has its header only. `history/cpi-tracking-backtest.csv` is the production engine’s back-cast of every month from January 2015 to September 2026: simulated, not live.

**Data.**
- BLS bulk files at download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/cu: the U.S. city average topic files (`cu.data.1`, `.2`, `.11` to `.18`, `.20`) for the indexes and `cu.aspect` for relative importance (aspect type I).
- FRED: the four regional owners’ equivalent rent indexes (`CUUR0100SEHC`, `CUUR0200SEHC`, `CUUR0300SEHC`, `CUUR0400SEHC`), and the Cleveland Fed’s median and trimmed-mean CPI (`MEDCPIM157SFRBCLE`, `TRMMEANCPIM157SFRBCLE`) for scoring.
- EIA open data: U.S. regular retail gasoline (`EMM_EPMR_PTE_NUS_DPG`), U.S. No. 2 diesel retail (`EMD_EPD2D_PTE_NUS_DPG`), both weekly, and the Henry Hub spot price (`RNGWHHD`), daily; each averaged by month.
- The Cleveland Fed’s current component weights (its median CPI component table, clevelandfed.org).
- `inputs/cpi_pieces.csv` (the 49 pieces and their cuts), `inputs/cpi_published_cuts.csv` (the BLS series each cut is scored against), `inputs/cleveland_median_cpi_components.csv` (the Cleveland Fed’s 45 components).

**How close.** Reproduces the production engine exactly. On November 8, 2026 the script’s back-cast matched the production engine’s on every one of the 141 months from January 2015 to September 2026, for all twelve cuts, to six decimals. The nightly check compares the ten cuts built from BLS’s pieces with `history/cpi-tracking-backtest.csv` over the latest 24 months, to 0.000001 percentage point. The median and trimmed mean are left out of the nightly comparison, though they matched too: their weights are re-anchored each month to the Cleveland Fed’s latest table and the seasonal factors for regional rent are re-estimated as months are added, so the back-cast of those two drifts a little from a file made on one day.

Two maintenance notes. Every February BLS revises five years of seasonal factors, which moves every recent month’s seasonally adjusted change; after that release the back-test file must be regenerated from the production engine, or the nightly check will (correctly) report the difference. And the test uses today’s seasonal factors for every past month, because vintages of the component factors are not published, so it flatters history slightly.

How it has scored, back-cast against BLS’s published figures (average miss in the one-month change, percentage points): core 0.049 in 2015–19, 0.074 since 2022 and 0.074 since 2024; all items 0.048, 0.084 and 0.071; median 0.043, 0.064 and 0.056; trimmed mean 0.041, 0.073 and 0.059. Core beats its own 12-month average clearly only in the volatile stretch since 2022 (0.074 against 0.085); since 2024 the two tie. Most of a month’s core miss is noise in the print: used cars, lodging, airfares, insurance.
