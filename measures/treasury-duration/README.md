# Treasury Duration Index

**On econvitals.org:** [Fiscal](https://econvitals.org/fiscal/), the full-width chart “Duration of Marketable Treasury Debt.” Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#treasury-duration).

**What it is.** Treasury publishes the average maturity of its debt but not its duration — how sensitive the government’s interest bill is to a change in rates — and nobody publishes either with the Federal Reserve’s holdings netted out. Econvitals builds all three lines from every individual Treasury security outstanding (the Monthly Statement of the Public Debt) matched to the New York Fed’s security-by-security holdings:

- **All marketable debt** — the par-weighted Macaulay duration of every marketable Treasury security at the month end.
- **Net of the Fed** — the Treasuries the Fed owns are treated as if they had been swapped, dollar for dollar, for the overnight reserves that fund them (a duration of one day). The denominator is unchanged.
- **Net of the Fed, including MBS** — also subtracts the dollar duration of the Fed’s agency mortgage-backed securities, agency debt and agency commercial MBS, funded the same way, over the same Treasury par. From December 2008.

Duration is weighted by each security’s face value (par), because that is what the government refinances, and is a real duration for inflation-protected securities (discounted on Treasury’s real yield curve). Bills are a single payment at maturity; floating-rate notes reset weekly, so their duration is seven days; notes and bonds are discounted at Treasury’s par yield curve interpolated to their own maturity. A guard refuses to go on unless every month’s securities add up, class by class, to Treasury’s own published subtotals within 0.05 percent.

The Fed’s MBS duration is a model, because nobody publishes it for the Fed’s book. Each pool group (coupon and maturity, from the New York Fed’s file) gets a prepayment rate: housing turnover of 3.5 to 7 percent a year, lower when the pool’s rate is far below the market’s, plus a refinancing S-curve in the gap between the pool’s rate (taken as coupon + 0.6) and Freddie Mac’s 30- or 15-year mortgage rate, halved for pools two or more years old. Cash flows are discounted at the Treasury par curve plus 0.8 point, and effective duration comes from shifting the curve and the mortgage rate together by ±25 basis points. Agency debt and agency commercial MBS are priced as plain bonds. MBS are about 7 percent of Treasury par, so a full year of error in MBS duration moves the third line by about 0.1 year.

These are house estimates. The chart runs from 2007; the MBS line starts in December 2008.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` rebuilds the last 36 months (about three minutes on a first run, since it downloads one month of each file at a time). `python replicate.py --since 2007-01` rebuilds the whole chart, but be warned: it downloads several gigabytes and takes an hour or more, because the Fed’s monthly agency holdings files for the years of its large MBS purchases run to 30–75 MB each. Needs a free `FRED_API_KEY` for the mortgage rates; Treasury and the New York Fed need no key. Writes `output/treasury_duration.csv`: `all_debt`, `net_fed`, `incl_mbs` in years, plus the par outstanding (`par_bn`), the Fed’s share (`fed_share_pct`) and the holdings date used (`soma_date`).

**Data.** All keyless except FRED:

- Treasury’s **Monthly Statement of the Public Debt, Table 3** (marketable securities), from the Fiscal Data API: `https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v1/debt/mspd/mspd_table_3_market`, one month end at a time. Each security’s maturity date, coupon and amount outstanding. Table 3 lists a security once per auction and reopening with the amount only on the first row; matured-but-unredeemed securities stay listed (they are dropped here); and before June 2004 TIPS are filed as “Inflation-Indexed Notes/Bonds.” The reader in `common/finance.py` handles all three.
- The New York Fed’s **SOMA holdings**, security by security: Treasuries from `https://markets.newyorkfed.org/api/soma/tsy/get/asof/<date>.json` and agency securities from `https://markets.newyorkfed.org/api/soma/agency/get/asof/<date>.json`, on the latest Wednesday at or before each month end (the list of dates is `https://markets.newyorkfed.org/api/soma/asofdates/list.json`). A few pre-2010 bills carry a different CUSIP in the Fed’s file than in Treasury’s; those are matched on maturity date and coupon. MBS coupons and maturities are read from the security description (e.g. “UMBS MORTPASS 2.5% 10/50”).
- Treasury’s **daily par and real yield curves**, one CSV per year from [home.treasury.gov](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve), on the month end (or the nearest business day before it) for Treasuries and on the holdings date for the Fed’s agency securities.
- Freddie Mac’s **Primary Mortgage Market Survey**, 30- and 15-year fixed rates, through FRED (`MORTGAGE30US`, `MORTGAGE15US`), averaged over the month of the holdings date. Econvitals reads the same weekly numbers from Freddie Mac’s own history file.

**How close.** Reproduces the published series exactly: checked every night over the last 36 months to within 0.0001 year (the site rounds to four decimals). Further back it matches too: every month from January 2007 through December 2013, and a sample of one month a year from 2014 to 2022, agree with the chart to all four decimals on all three lines. Like the site, the script rounds the net-of-the-Fed line to four decimals before subtracting the MBS leg. The newest month appears a few days after it ends, once Treasury posts that month’s statement.
