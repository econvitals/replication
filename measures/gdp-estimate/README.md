# Econvitals Current-Quarter GDP Estimate

**On econvitals.org:** [Home › GDP](https://econvitals.org/#gdp) (the Econvitals estimate card), [GDP › Current-quarter tracker: track record](https://econvitals.org/gdp/#tracker) (its own column) and the [GDP Trackers tool](https://econvitals.org/tools/gdp-trackers/) (components, path and accuracy). Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#gdp-estimate).

**What it is.** A mechanical estimate of what BEA’s advance GDP release will say, built the way BEA builds it. Each component (consumer goods and services, equipment, structures, housing, government, inventories and trade) is taken from the same monthly source data BEA uses (monthly PCE, retail sales, factory shipments, construction spending, the trade report and payrolls), translated into what BEA prints by a regression on BEA’s own history since 2012, weighted by its share of GDP and summed. A month BEA will not have on release day is filled by a stated rule: the trailing-12-month trend for the growth lines, the last level for inventories and trade; federal spending and intellectual property, which have no monthly reader, continue at the average of their last four published quarterly growth rates. Because BEA’s first print has on average come in about 0.35 percentage point below today’s revised figures, that gap is added back. In a real-time back-test over 47 quarters the error on the last day before the release averaged 0.91 point, against 0.75 for GDPNow, and it beat GDPNow one to two weeks out. It has been a leg of the [composite tracker](../composite-gdp-tracker/) since October 19, 2026, at a fixed 10% weight.

**What this folder rebuilds, and why not more.** Not the model. A faithful rebuild means ten bridge equations, each refitted whenever BEA revises its history, on about forty monthly source series from BEA, the Census Bureau and BLS, each cut to what had been published on the day by a release calendar; four inventory books deflated separately; trade less nonmonetary gold; and one source (capital goods imports by end use) seasonally adjusted in-house. That is far longer than a readable standalone script, and a shortened version would be a different model. What is checked instead:

- **The first-print adjustment.** `python replicate.py` (needs a free `FRED_API_KEY`) rebuilds it from public data: for every quarter since 2011 at least four quarters before the one being estimated (so each has been through an annual update), BEA’s advance estimate, as the Atlanta Fed records it beside GDPNow, minus today’s BEA figure, leaving out 2020 Q2 and Q3. It writes `output/first_print_gaps.csv` and prints the mean beside the adjustment in the estimate the site currently shows (read from `econvitals.org/tools/gdp-trackers/data.json`). For the fourth quarter of 2026 it gets −0.3560 point against the site’s −0.3556. The difference is one quarter: the site leaves out the third quarter of 2024, because the Atlanta Fed’s track record misdates that quarter’s advance estimate (September 30 rather than October 30, 2024) and Econvitals’ reader skips a row whose date comes before the quarter could have been published. The advance estimate on that row is right, so this script keeps it.
- **Its daily readings.** The estimate’s published value each day enters the composite tracker’s nightly check as a recorded input, and the composite reproduces.

**Data.** The Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow workbook (sheet `TrackRecord`: BEA’s advance estimates, unrounded) and FRED `A191RL1Q225SBEA` (real GDP growth, today’s vintage, BEA table 1.1.1).

**How close.** Not compared nightly (status “documented”). The first-print adjustment agrees to 0.0004 point, for the reason above.
