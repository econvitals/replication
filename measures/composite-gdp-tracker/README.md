# Econvitals Composite GDP Tracker

**On econvitals.org:** [Home › GDP](https://econvitals.org/#gdp) (the composite card), [GDP](https://econvitals.org/gdp/#gdp) (the nowcast strip, the Actual/Tracker toggle and the tracker charts), [GDP › Current-quarter tracker: track record](https://econvitals.org/gdp/#tracker), the [GDP Trackers tool](https://econvitals.org/tools/gdp-trackers/) and [What Forecasters Expect](https://econvitals.org/tools/forecasts/). Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#composite-gdp-tracker).

**What it is.** One number for the quarter in progress, blended from four trackers: the Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow, the New York Fed Staff Nowcast, the median of Wall Street tracking estimates (entered as one leg so no single firm can move it) and the [Econvitals Current-Quarter GDP Estimate](../gdp-estimate/). The weights are fixed by how many days remain until BEA’s advance estimate rather than fitted: the Econvitals estimate always takes 10%; more than 45 days out (or before BEA has scheduled the release) the other legs share the rest equally; from 15 to 45 days GDPNow takes half of the rest; inside 15 days it takes 60%. Between a leg’s updates it is nudged by half of the move in the Econvitals estimate since its last reading, so a tracker that has not yet reacted to a release does not drag the blend. The band is GDPNow’s own average miss at that horizon since 2014 (±1.4 points 61 days or more out, 1.1 from 31, 0.9 from 15, 0.85 from 8, 0.75 in the last week). Each quarter the composite and every leg are scored against BEA’s advance estimate. The weights are a house rule chosen from back-tests, not estimated.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (no key needed). It rebuilds the composite for every day econvitals.org has published one and writes `output/composite.csv` (one row per day per quarter: each leg, its weight, the composite and the band), then rebuilds the back-test beside the track record and writes `output/backtest.csv`.

`python history.py` (no key needed) writes the downloadable track record to `history/` at the top of this repository: `gdp-tracking.csv` (every published day), `gdp-tracking-scored.csv` (every quarter against BEA’s advance estimate) and `gdp-tracking-backtest.csv` (the back-test). See `history/README.md`.

**Data, and what is recorded rather than fetched.**
- GDPNow: the Atlanta Fed’s workbook, `atlantafed.org/-/media/Project/Atlanta/FRBA/Documents/research-and-data/data/gdpnow/GDPTrackingModelDataAndForecasts.xlsx` (sheet `TrackingHistory` for the quarter in progress, `TrackingArchives` for earlier quarters, `TrackRecord` for advance estimates).
- New York Fed Staff Nowcast: `newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/Research/Interactives/Data/NowCast/Downloads/New-York-Fed-Staff-Nowcast_download_data.xlsx` (sheet “Forecasts By Quarter”).
- Recorded inputs, from Econvitals’ published daily history (`data/gdp-nowcast-history.json` in the econvitals-data repository): the Street median, which comes from bank research notes that are not public and is used only as the site published it; the Econvitals estimate as it stood each day (its own folder explains why the model is not rebuilt here); and three facts about each day’s snapshot: the days left to the advance estimate, which legs were present, and the date of each Fed model’s latest reading when the snapshot was taken. The script fetches each Fed model’s reading for that date itself, applies the nudge from the recorded path of the Econvitals estimate, and recomputes the weights, the composite and the band.

**How close.** The composite reproduces to within 0.0002 point on every day since October 28, 2026, when Econvitals began storing the legs to four decimals; for the first week of the record (October 19–27) the published legs were rounded to two decimals and the composite agrees to within 0.006 point. GDPNow and the New York Fed legs agree to the same 0.0002, the weights exactly (to the three decimals published) and the band exactly. The live comparison is against `history/gdp-tracking.csv`, so rerunning `history.py` extends it.

The script also lists, for each day, the latest reading each Fed had published by that date (`*_latest_published`). From October 29 through November 5, 2026 the published composite for the fourth quarter left out the New York Fed leg, although the New York Fed’s file carried fourth-quarter readings (2.81 percent as of October 23, 2.9 as of October 30); with it, the weights would have been 45/45/10 rather than 90/10. The rebuild follows the published snapshots there.

**The back-test.** The track record’s pre-history: GDPNow, the New York Fed Staff Nowcast and their plain average on the New York Fed’s last Friday before each advance estimate since 2023, from the two Feds’ own archives (not the live weighting). The script reproduces the published rows to their two decimals. Like the published back-test, it takes each advance estimate’s date from the Atlanta Fed’s `TrackRecord` sheet. That sheet gives September 30 as the release date for the third quarters of 2024 and 2026, which BEA actually published on October 30, 2024 and October 29, 2026 (the dates the same workbook’s `TrackingArchives` sheet carries). So the published Q3 2024 row uses readings from September 27, a month early; on the right date (October 25) the plain average is 3.11 percent rather than 3.03. The script reproduces the published row and adds the corrected figures in `note`, `as_of_on_release_date` and `average_on_release_date`. The Q3 2026 row is not in the published back-test; the script prints it with the same flag.
