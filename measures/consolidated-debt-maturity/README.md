# Consolidated Debt Maturity

**On econvitals.org:** [Fiscal](https://econvitals.org/fiscal/), the cross-country chart “Average Maturity of Government Debt, Gross and Net of the Central Bank.” Method note: [Methods](https://econvitals.org/methods/#consolidated-debt-maturity).

**What it is.** The international companion to the [Treasury duration index](../treasury-duration/): for 18 economies, the average maturity of the government’s marketable debt as issued, and again with the central bank’s holdings of it counted as overnight money — the maturity of the consolidated government-plus-central-bank balance sheet. With D the marketable debt, WAM its average maturity, CB the central bank’s holdings and WAM_CB their average maturity:

> net = (D × WAM − CB × WAM_CB) / D

The denominator stays D: the consolidated government owes the same amount either way; only the maturity changes.

It is a one-time end-2025 snapshot. Euro-area countries are general government (the European Central Bank’s data) while the others are central government. Switzerland’s net figure is a bound, because its central bank does not disclose the maturity of its holdings.

**Run it.** `python replicate.py` (no key needed). Writes `output/debt_maturity.csv`: `gross` and `net_cb` as the chart shows them (two decimals; one for Switzerland, whose debt office publishes its average maturity to one decimal), the unrounded `gross_exact` and `net_exact`, and `basis`, which says how each row was built.

**Data, row by row.**

- **United States — computed here.** Every marketable Treasury security outstanding on 31 December 2025, from Treasury’s Monthly Statement of the Public Debt, Table 3 (Fiscal Data API, `v1/debt/mspd/mspd_table_3_market`), matched to the New York Fed’s SOMA holdings of 31 December 2025 (`markets.newyorkfed.org/api/soma/tsy/get/asof/2025-12-31.json`). Par-weighted years to maturity, bills included; net of the Fed, its holdings get a maturity of one day. This is the same security-level reader the duration index uses (`common/finance.py`).
- **Euro area and ten member countries — computed here** from the European Central Bank:
  - D and WAM: government finance statistics, general government debt securities, face value, December 2025 — the outstanding amount (`GFS/M.N..W0.S13.S1.N.L.LE.F3.T._Z.EUR._T.F.V.N._T`) and the average residual maturity (`GFS/M.N..W0.S13.S1.N.L.LE.F3.TT._Z.YR._T.F.V.A1._T`) from the [ECB data portal API](https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/). “Euro area” is the ECB’s 20-country aggregate (`I9`).
  - CB and WAM_CB: the Eurosystem’s holdings under the public sector purchase programme and the pandemic programme, by issuer country, from the ECB’s breakdown files ([PSPP](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/pdf/PSPP_breakdown_history.csv), [PEPP](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/pdf/PEPP_public_sector_securities_breakdown_history.csv)): monthly net purchases summed through December 2025, and the published average maturity of each country’s holdings. Those sums are at acquisition cost less redemptions, so each programme is scaled to amortized cost with its own end-2025 ratio, from the programme-level holdings files ([APP](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/pdf/APP_breakdown_history.csv), [PEPP](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/pdf/PEPP_breakdown_history.csv)) — about 0.905 for PSPP and 0.952 for PEPP. For the euro area as a whole, holdings of supranational bonds are left out. Holdings include national agencies’ bonds (KfW in Germany, for example), which are not general government, so the central bank’s share is somewhat overstated.
- **Japan, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Sweden, Switzerland — recorded inputs.** `inputs/recorded_inputs.csv` gives D, WAM, CB and WAM_CB for each, with the debt office’s and central bank’s documents they come from. These were assembled by hand, issue by issue, from lists published as PDFs, spreadsheets with changing layouts, or (Australia’s) a site that refuses scripted downloads, so the script does the arithmetic from them rather than refetching. The figures are public statistics; the file is Econvitals’ own compilation of them. Japan’s debt is general bonds (the Ministry of Finance’s average maturity of 9 years 5 months) plus Financing Bills; the United Kingdom’s average maturity is the Debt Management Office’s published 13.46 years for gilts and bills, with the Bank of England’s Asset Purchase Facility at face value; Switzerland uses the Swiss National Bank’s franc bond portfolio duration (8.1 years) as a stand-in for the undisclosed maturity of its holdings, which matter little either way (any value from 0 to 30 years gives 10.6 to 10.9).

**How close.** Every gross figure and every net figure but one match the chart to the cent; checked every night within 0.005 year, the rounding of the chart’s two decimals. The exception is the **U.S. net figure**: this script computes **4.66** years, and the chart shows **4.69**. 4.66 is also what Econvitals’ own security-level maturity series shows for December 2025 (4.6616, the “Netting out the Fed” line of the chart “Average Maturity of Marketable Treasury Debt,” published as `data/treasury-debt-maturity.json`), and the method says the U.S. row is that series, so the chart’s 4.69 appears to be a slip in the hand-assembled table; until it is corrected the nightly check reports this row as not matching. The ECB’s December 2025 figures can still be revised; if they are, the euro-area rows here will move while the frozen chart does not.
