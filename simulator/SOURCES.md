# Sources

## The Federal Reserve Board’s FRB/US packages (`fed/`)

The model and its data are the Board’s own public release, bundled exactly as used, because the Board replaces these files on its website without keeping old versions (it replaced the data package on November 4, 2026), and a fresh download gives a different baseline and different numbers. `fed/VINTAGE.json` records each file’s address, date and size.

* **`fed/pyfrbus/`: pyfrbus 1.1.1**, the Board’s Python platform for FRB/US, with the model itself in `models/model.xml`. Downloaded from <https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/files/pyfrbus.zip>, dated February 26, 2026. Unmodified; only the files that installing it creates were left out. Public domain: it was written by U.S. Government employees on official time (`fed/pyfrbus/LICENSE`).
* **`fed/data_only_package/`: the FRB/US data package**, `LONGBASE.TXT` (history through 2026Q2 and the Board staff’s projection and long-run extrapolation beyond it) and its `README.TXT`. Downloaded from <https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/files/data_only_package.zip>, dated November 4, 2026. `HISTDATA.TXT` and the EViews database in the same download are left out because the engine does not read them. This is the Board’s public release, but not all of it is public domain: as its `README.TXT` notes, the history of the Baa corporate bond yield (`rbbb`) is Moody’s Seasoned Baa Corporate Bond Yield, from Bloomberg Finance LP.

Documentation: the Board’s [FRB/US project page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-about.htm), its [Python package page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-python.htm) and [data package page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-package.htm); Brayton, Laubach and Reifschneider, “[The FRB/US Model: A Tool for Macroeconomic Policy Analysis](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econresdata/notes/feds-notes/2014/a-tool-for-macroeconomic-policy-analysis.html)” (FEDS Notes, April 2014); the Board’s page on [policy rules](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/policy-rules-and-how-policymakers-use-them.htm). The simulations in this package were not made by the Federal Reserve, which has not reviewed or endorsed them.

## Inputs typed into the engine (`inputs/` holds a copy)

Every other number the engine uses is typed into its code, each with its source in a comment beside it, so nothing is downloaded when it runs. They are the tariff’s parameters; `inputs/tariff_parameters.csv` is a copy of them, written out from the code when this folder was exported, for anyone who wants them without reading Python; the engine never reads `inputs/`. Every other shock is defined in the model’s own variables (`METHOD.md`).

| Input | Where in the code | Source |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Capital goods’ share of tariff duties (0.29) | `tariff.py`: `CAPITAL_SHARE` | U.S. Census Bureau, [international trade](https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/data/index.html), imports for consumption by end-use category (customs value and calculated duties), read November 8, 2026: capital goods except automotive were 29.2 percent of 2024 goods imports and 27.8 percent of the extra duties collected from January 2025 to September 2026 over the 2024 rate |
| Timing of tariff pass-through to consumer prices (0.25, 0.45, 0.30 over three quarters) | `tariff.py`: `LAG_WEIGHTS` | Minton, Ray and Somale, “[Detecting Tariff Effects on Consumer Prices in Real Time, Part II](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/detecting-tariff-effects-on-consumer-prices-in-real-time-part-II-20260408.html)” (FEDS Notes, April 8, 2026): about half of a tariff reaches consumer prices in three months, all of it in about seven |
| Share of the duties paid by consumers (0.5) | `tariff.py`: `PASS_THROUGH` | Calibrated so that FRB/US’s effect of the 2025–26 tariffs on core PCE inflation over the four quarters to 2026Q1 is about 0.6 point, the middle of the published range of 0.4 to 0.8: Federal Reserve Board staff (Minton, Ray and Somale, above: 0.8 percent on the core PCE price level through February 2026, 0.4 without time effects); Chair Powell’s [press conference of March 18, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/mediacenter/files/FOMCpresconf20260318.pdf) (“between a half and three-quarters” of about 3 percent core inflation); the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, “[How Tariffs Are Affecting Prices in 2025](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/oct/how-tariffs-are-affecting-prices-2025)” (0.4 point of annualized core PCE inflation over June–August 2025); the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, “[Initially delayed, the pass-through of tariffs to consumer prices has arrived](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/initially-delayed-the-pass-through-of-tariffs-to-consumer-prices-has-arrived)” (0.2 to 0.4 point as of July 2026) |
| Share of the duty on capital goods paid by businesses buying equipment (0.5) | `tariff.py`: `EQUIPMENT_PASS_THROUGH` | An assumption, set equal to the consumer share, so that companies bear half of the duties in all (the rest comes out of profits, `PROFIT_SHARE`) |

The business-investment shock’s model file is not an input: `engine/invest_model.py` writes it from the Board’s `model.xml` the first time it is needed (`fed/model_invest.xml`).
