# Frozen inputs

Every file here is public data, saved as it was used for the post and its online appendix (November 2026). Nothing in the package goes to the network. Series codes are in each file’s column headers, in square brackets.

| File | What it is | Source and vintage | Used for |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| `z1_series.csv` | Households and nonprofits: net worth, real estate, owner-occupied real estate, home mortgages, owners’ equity, durables, consumer credit, directly held and all (direct and indirect) corporate equities, noncorporate equity, deposits; revaluations of net worth, real estate, directly held equities and mutual funds. Quarterly, end of quarter, millions of dollars, 1951Q4–2026Q2 | Federal Reserve Board, Financial Accounts of the United States (Z.1), release of September 11, 2026, tables S1M_b (formerly B.101), S1M_e_b (B.101.e) and S1M_r (R.101): [federalreserve.gov/releases/z1](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/) | the wealth ratios and holding gains in every regression |
| `bea_series.csv` | Personal consumption expenditures, disposable income, outlays, saving and the saving rate, compensation, transfers, interest and dividend income, proprietors’ and rental income, contributions, personal taxes, personal income, population; PCE price indexes (total, core, by type); PCE by type. Quarterly, seasonally adjusted annual rates, millions of dollars | BEA, NIPA Tables 2.1, 2.3.4 and 2.3.5, through 2026Q3 (advance estimate): [apps.bea.gov/iTable](https://apps.bea.gov/iTable/?reqid=19) | spending relative to income, transfers, income growth, real values |
| `fred_series_quarterly.csv` | Unemployment rate, 3-month Treasury bill, 10-year Treasury, federal funds rate, Michigan sentiment, banks’ willingness to lend, Baa yield; quarterly averages of the monthly series | FRED (`UNRATE`, `TB3MS`, `GS10`, `FEDFUNDS`, `UMCSENT`, `DRIWCIL`, `BAA`), November 2026 | the unemployment and real-interest-rate controls; sentiment in one sensitivity test |
| `bea_real_pce.csv` | Real PCE, chained 2017 dollars | BEA NIPA Table 2.3.6 (`DPCERX`), through 2026Q3 | the FRB/US comparison in real terms |
| `frbus/HISTDATA.TXT` | FRB/US historical database | Federal Reserve Board, FRB/US data package of November 4, 2026 (history through 2026Q2): [federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-about.htm](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-about.htm); public domain. `README_board.TXT` is the Board’s own description | FRB/US’s consumption equations run from 2019 (post Figure 5; appendix Section 6.2, Table A19) |
| `simulator/stocks_earn_var_no_fixed.json`, `simulator/stocks_var_no_fixed.json`, `simulator/current.json` | The Econvitals Macroeconomic Simulator’s stored FRB/US runs for a permanent 30 percent fall in stock prices (backward-looking expectations, balanced-approach rule, taxes fixed), as deviations from the Board’s baseline; and the factor (1.34) that scales them to today’s household stock wealth | [econvitals.org/tools/macro-simulator](https://econvitals.org/tools/macro-simulator/); the runs are produced by the Simulator’s engine, whose replication is this repository’s `simulator/` folder. Subset of the stored files: the one run the post uses | Table 1’s FRB/US columns; Figure 7; the 0.9 and 1.4 cents |
| `bea_gdp_quarterly.csv` | Real GDP and real PCE (chained 2017 dollars), nominal GDP, and the quantity index for business fixed investment | BEA NIPA Tables 1.1.6, 1.1.5 and 1.1.3 (`A191RX`, `DPCERX`, `A191RC`, `A008RA`), through 2026Q3 | 2001 and 1987–88 growth, stock wealth relative to GDP, Table 1’s GDP |
| `bea_t10102_contributions.csv` | Contributions to percent change in real GDP by component, quarterly | BEA NIPA Table 1.1.2, as downloaded November 15, 2026 | Figure 2 and the consumer share of each recession’s shortfall |
| `fred_unrate.csv`, `fred_cpiaucsl.csv`, `fred_ussthpi.csv`, `fred_fed_funds_target_2000_2002.csv` | Monthly unemployment rate; CPI; FHFA all-transactions house price index (quarterly); the federal funds target, daily, 2000–2002 | FRED (`UNRATE`, `CPIAUCSL`, `USSTHPI`, `DFEDTAR`/`DFEDTARU`), November 2026 | 2001 (unemployment 3.9 to 5.7, the Fed’s cuts from 6.5 to 1.75); house prices over each stock decline |
| `z1_f511_corporate_equities.csv` | Corporate equities at market value: nonfinancial (`LM103164105`) and financial (`LM793164105`) issuers, closed-end fund (`LM554090005`) and ETF (`LM564090005`) shares, and rest-of-world holdings (`LM263064105`) | Financial Accounts table F.51.1.s (formerly L.223), release of September 11, 2026 | Figure 4: all U.S. corporate stock (including closely held, excluding fund shares) relative to GDP; the foreign share |
| `dfa_corporate_equities_and_mutual_funds_by_wealth.csv` | Corporate equities and mutual fund shares held by the bottom 50 percent, next 40, next 9, rest of the top 1 and top 0.1 percent of households by wealth, quarterly, 1989Q3–2026Q2, millions of dollars | Federal Reserve Board, Distributional Financial Accounts (network levels), November 2026: [federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/dataviz/dfa](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/dataviz/dfa/) | the top 0.1 and top 10 percent shares |
| `forbes_top10.csv` | Net worth of the ten richest Americans in September 2026, and the same ten people in October 2019 | Forbes 400, October 2019 and September 2026, typed from forbes.com; Jensen Huang’s 2019 value inferred from Forbes’s 2020 list (see the file) | the $2.5 trillion and the $7 billion each |
| `french_factors_daily.csv` | Daily Fama–French factors (Mkt-RF and RF give the whole market’s value-weighted return), through September 30, 2026 | Kenneth R. French data library (CRSP data), as used for Part 2 of the series (same file as `posts/2026-11-15-how-far-could-stocks-fall/data/`) | Figure 1: declines of 20 percent or more since 1946 |
| `shiller_dividend_yield_as_used.csv` | The S&P Composite’s dividend yield, monthly | Robert Shiller’s `ie_data.xls`, the copy downloaded November 14, 2026 (dividends through June 2024; later months use the June 2024 yield) — same file as Part 2’s | turning French’s total return into a price-only index |
| `greenbook/r*.xlsx` | Real-time vintages of real GDP, consumption, residential and business investment and government purchases | Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia, Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists: [philadelphiafed.org](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/real-time-data-set-for-macroeconomists), downloaded November 16, 2026 | Figure 3: 2001 as first published (February 2002) and as now measured |

**Typed from documents** (in the code, not in files): the December 13, 2000 Greenbook’s forecasts for 2001, fourth quarter over fourth quarter (Part 1, p. I-6: GDP 3.1, consumer spending 3.0, residential investment −0.4, business investment 7.1, government 3.6; [Philadelphia Fed Greenbook archive](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/philadelphia-fed-greenbook-data-set)), in `wealth/post_numbers.py`; NBER business-cycle peaks, in the same file; and the published papers’ estimates in the appendix’s literature table (Table A1).

**Not included:** the S&P 500 index (licensed; see `licensed/README.md`) and the county study’s 10 GB of inputs (see `county/README.md`).
